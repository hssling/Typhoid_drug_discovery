# 📄 Phase 14: Grant Specific Aims (v4.0 Mastery)

**Project Title**: Convergent Multi-Omics and AI-Driven Discovery of Broad-Spectrum Host-Directed Therapies for Enteric Fever.

## Executive Summary
Enteric fever, caused by *Salmonella Typhi* and *Paratyphi A*, remains a major global health threat, exacerbated by the rise of extensively drug-resistant (XDR) strains. Current antibiotic-centric strategies are failing. This project leverages an authentic v4.0 multi-omics discovery engine to identify host-directed therapies (HDT) that enhance the innate immune response to Salmonella, providing an AMR-resilient treatment paradigm.

---

## Specific Aim 1: High-Throughput Validation of AI-Predicted Host Targets and Ligands.
We have identified a set of high-priority host targets (e.g., `IL1B`, `NLRP3`, `MTOR`) and prioritized over 200 repurposing candidates using a Graph-inspired Neural Network (GNN-MLP). 
- **1a**: We will calibrate the AI prediction models using an *in vitro* high-throughput screening (HTS) dataset of 10,000 compounds to refine pChEMBL affinity coefficients.
- **1b**: We will perform dose-response validation of Top 5 candidates in human THP-1 macrophage models to satisfy Proof-of-Mechanism (PoM) metrics (v4.0 Validation Protocol).

## Specific Aim 2: Pre-clinical Evaluation of Broad-Spectrum Efficacy in Enteric Organoid Models.
Global impact requires a therapy that works across the enteric spectrum (Typhoid + Paratyphoid + NTS).
- **2a**: We will utilize human gut-on-a-chip and intestinal organoid models to evaluate the efficacy of conserved HDT hubs (identified in v4.0 Pan-Enteric Scaling) against multiple Salmonella serovars.
- **2b**: We will define the translational pharmacokinetics (PK) and safety profiles of prioritized HDTs to establish a Target Product Profile (TPP) for Phase I clinical trials.

---
## Impact Statement
Successful completion of these aims will deliver the first **Causally-Verified, AI-Validated Host-Directed clinical candidate** for Enteric Fever, providing a critical safety net against the post-antibiotic era.
